# Results

Perovskite FESEM microdefect detection — synthetic-to-real study.
All numbers below are reproducible from `run_full_pipeline.py`; every figure is
regenerated by a numbered stage. 36 tests passing.

---

## 0. Read this first — every synthetic arm below trained on PbI₂ only

Found 2026-09-06 while checking why a synthetic-only run scored 0.0000 on
real validation. `data/yolo_export.py` selected its synthetic sample as a
**prefix** of the pool manifest, `entries[:N]`. The merged pool lists all
5,000 `syn_*_pbi2_*` images before all 5,000 `syn_*_pinhole_*` images, so a
5% sample was 500 PbI₂ images and **zero pinholes**. Verified on disk for
every export:

| regime | synthetic files | synthetic boxes, by class |
|---|---|---|
| `scale_002` | 200 | pbi2 10,371 · pinhole **0** |
| `scale_005` (old headline, §1) | 500 | pbi2 25,908 · pinhole **0** |
| `scale_005_refined_nofft` (H2, §3) | 500 | pbi2 25,908 · pinhole **0** |
| `scale_005_steps2000` (step-matched, §13) | 500 | pbi2 25,908 · pinhole **0** |
| `scale_010` | 1,000 | pbi2 51,816 · pinhole **0** |
| `scale_025` | 2,500 | pbi2 129,481 · pinhole **0** |

The test split is 55% pinhole boxes by count and 87% of *real* training boxes
are pinhole. Every "does synthetic data help" number in §1–§3 and §13 is
therefore a measurement of adding **PbI₂-only** synthetic images to a
pinhole-dominated task. Read them as that. In particular:

- The scaling ladder's shape (§2: "the optimum is low, more is worse") is what
  adding thousands of images of the *minority* class does to a detector whose
  metric is dominated by the majority class. It is not evidence about
  synthetic-data volume.
- The step-matched null (§13) stands, and is if anything stronger: the old 2×
  came from PbI₂-only images at 4× the compute.
- The H2 ablation (§3) compared two PbI₂-only arms.
- The synthetic-only arm scoring 0.0000 (§15) is a detector that never saw a
  pinhole being asked to find pinholes. It is not a transfer result.

The sampling is fixed to an even stride through the manifest (250 PbI₂ + 250
pinhole images at 5%), the class mix is now written into every export
manifest as `synthetic_boxes_per_class`, and `tests/test_yolo_export.py`
fails if a sample ever comes back single-class again. The pool itself was
always balanced; the bug was in what was taken from it. Which arms are
re-run with balanced synthetic is recorded in §15–§17 as they land; every
number above is kept, labelled, because the checkpoints exist and the runs
were real.

## 1. Headline — locked test set

**At a matched optimisation budget, adding 5% GAN-refined synthetic data does
not improve detection on unseen real FESEM images. The 2× reported in earlier
drafts was the extra gradient steps the synthetic images brought with them,
not the images.**

Three arms, n = 3 seeds each (1, 2, 42), YOLO11s @ 640 px, scored once on the
locked test split:

| arm | steps | epochs | mAP50 | mAP50-95 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| real only | 2,000 | 100 | 0.0688 ± 0.0105 | 0.0261 ± 0.0053 |
| + 5% synthetic, **step-matched** | **2,000** | 24 | **0.0662 ± 0.0186** | **0.0253 ± 0.0056** |
| + 5% synthetic, epoch-matched | 8,300 | 100 | 0.1333 ± 0.0128 | 0.0521 ± 0.0065 |

| comparison | ratio | Welch *p* | reads as |
|---|---|---|---|
| step-matched: synthetic@2,000 vs real@2,000 | **0.96×** | **0.84** | no effect |
| epoch-matched: synthetic@8,300 vs real@2,000 | 1.94× | 0.0028 | 4.15× the compute |

The epoch-matched row is the number this document used to lead with. It is
real, reproducible, and it measures the wrong thing: both arms ran 100 epochs,
but 660 training images against 160 means the synthetic arm took 8,300
gradient steps to the baseline's 2,000. Holding steps equal instead (§13)
removes the entire gain. The pre-registered reading for that outcome — written
in §13 before the run — was *"the gain was compute; §1 must be rewritten"*,
and this is that rewrite.

What this leaves standing, all still measured on the locked test split:

- The **corpus and protocol** (§11): leakage-safe, de-duplicated, 36 leaked
  groups in the published split quantified.
- The **generator** (§4, §5): label-exact by construction, severity monotonic in
  contrast (ρ = 0.9), geometry matched to the real box distribution.
- The **scaling-ladder shape** (§2): the epoch-matched optimum sits at 5%, and
  more synthetic data is worse — that ordering is unaffected by the confound
  because every rung carries it. What changes is that the *height* of the
  ladder is no longer evidence of transfer.
- The **H2 result** (§3): the FFT branch cuts the frequency gap 62% and buys
  nothing at detection — a distribution distance failing to predict utility,
  which §8.2 of the plan pre-registered as reportable.

### Per scale bin, at matched steps: no pattern

Mean over 3 seeds, AP50 on the locked test split. The earlier draft's table
compared real@2,000 against synthetic@8,300 and found a gain that fell
monotonically with defect size, +151% at T2 down to +28% at T4. At matched
steps that structure is gone:

| scale bin | n gt | AP50 real@2,000 | AP50 synth@2,000 | Δ | AP50 synth@8,300 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| T1 sub-stride (<8 px) | 249 | 0.0096 | 0.0067 | −30% | 0.0205 |
| T2 tiny (8–16 px) | 561 | 0.0561 | 0.0589 | +5% | 0.1410 |
| T3 small (16–32 px) | 340 | 0.1084 | 0.1204 | +11% | 0.1971 |
| T4 medium+ (≥32 px) | 181 | 0.1187 | 0.1000 | −16% | 0.1523 |

Two bins up, two down, none outside seed spread. The right-hand column is what
4.15× the steps does to every bin roughly alike, which is what a compute
effect looks like.

> **Corrections, kept in order.** An earlier draft printed recall under an
> `AP50` header (+14/+26/+6/−4%); those were recall deltas. The draft after
> that fixed the column and reported +114/+151/+82/+28% as a scale-dependent
> transfer effect. This draft reports that the effect was optimisation steps.
> Each earlier number is reproducible from its checkpoint; none survives the
> control.

## 2. Scaling ladder — the optimum is low

Seed 42, 100 epochs, batch 8 throughout. Step counts are
`ceil(train_imgs / 8) × 100`, verified against each export manifest.

| synthetic ratio | synth imgs | train imgs | steps | mAP50 | vs base |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0% | 0 | 160 | 2,000 | 0.0513 | 1.00× |
| 2% | 200 | 360 | 4,500 | 0.1020 | 1.99× |
| **5%** | **500** | **660** | **8,300** | **0.1249** | **2.44×** |
| 10% | 1,000 | 1,160 | 14,500 | 0.1010 | 1.97× |
| 25% | 2,500 | 2,660 | 33,300 | 0.1002 | 1.95× |

*(single seed — the 5% point is n=3 elsewhere; the curve **shape** is n=1)*

### What the shape does and does not prove

The **falling limb is clean.** 25% used **4.0× more** steps than 5% (33,300 vs
8,300) and performed **worse** (0.1002 vs 0.1249). More data *and* more compute
producing a worse detector cannot be a compute artifact, and it is the direct
evidence that this project's early failure — 98% synthetic, ~20× past the
optimum — was a ratio problem rather than a synthesis-quality problem.

The **rising limb is confounded.** Steps climb monotonically with ratio
(2,000 → 4,500 → 8,300) exactly as mAP50 climbs (0.0513 → 0.1020 → 0.1249), so
0%→5% cannot be separated from optimisation budget by this table alone. The
step-matched baseline in §13 is what settles it.

> **Corrections to an earlier version of this table.** The 0% row read
> `0.0583 / 6,000 steps`; both belonged to a superseded 300-epoch run that the
> append-only CSV had left behind. The 100-epoch value is **0.0513 / 2,000
> steps**, which raises every ratio. It also invalidates a claim made here
> earlier — that 2% used *fewer* steps than baseline. It used 2.25× **more**.
> That argument is withdrawn; the falling-limb argument above is unaffected and
> was always the stronger of the two.

## 3. H2 — does the FFT discriminator branch help?

**Short answer: it helps the generator on the axis it targets, and that does
not reach the detector.** Three lines of evidence, and the one ranked highest by
the pre-declared metric hierarchy is null.

### The line that matters most — detection utility (null)

Same pool ratio, same seed, same 100-epoch protocol; the FFT branch of the
refiner discriminator is the only difference.

| refiner discriminator | val mAP50 | val mAP50-95 |
|---|---|---|
| spatial ⊕ **FFT** (`scale_005_seed1`) | 0.1015 | 0.0419 |
| spatial only (`scale_005_refined_nofft_seed1`) | **0.1065** | **0.0464** |
| difference | **+0.0050 in favour of NO FFT** | +0.0045 |

The FFT branch did **not** improve detection, and the sign is against it. The
difference is far inside seed noise — the three `scale_005` seeds span
0.1015–0.1249 (Δ 0.023), i.e. **4.7× the size of this effect** — so the honest
reading is **no measurable detection-level effect**, not a win for spatial-only.
n=1 per arm; a 3-seed replication would cost ~2 h and is not worth it to
resolve an effect this far below the noise floor.

### The lines that favour it — generator-side only

**Frequency-domain gap** (`stage 10`, 40 real vs 40 synthetic per pool):

| pool | L1 pixel | **L2 frequency** | L3 morphology |
|---|---|---|---|
| renderer only | 6.87 | 0.218 | 1.185 |
| refined, **FFT on** | 5.13 | **0.203** | 1.210 |
| refined, **FFT off** | 4.56 | **0.530** | 1.180 |

The FFT branch cuts the frequency gap **~62%** — measured on exactly the axis
the branch targets, and independent of any detection experiment.

**Training stability:** FFT-on had **0/30** discriminator-saturated epochs;
FFT-off had **11/30**. Consistent across all four refiner runs this session.

> Caveat: FFT-off achieved *lower* reconstruction loss (rec 0.0402 vs 0.0458).
> `rec` is pixel reconstruction, not frequency realism, so it is the wrong
> metric for H2 — but it is reported rather than omitted.

### What this means for the domain-gap → utility question

This is a clean instance of the effect §8.2 of the plan was built to detect. The
FFT branch cut the **L2 frequency gap by 62%** and the detector did not care.

**A large, targeted, measurable reduction in a distribution distance produced no
downstream utility gain.** The metric hierarchy was declared before any of these
numbers existed and puts real detection gain above frequency consistency
precisely so this could not be reported the other way round. So:

- H2 is **not supported** at the level that matters.
- The FFT branch is retained anyway for a different, defensible reason: it
  eliminated discriminator saturation (0/30 vs 11/30 epochs), which is a
  training-stability argument, not a realism one.
- More broadly, this is evidence **against** the common practice of selecting a
  generative model by distribution distance to real data. On this corpus the
  frequency gap was actively misleading.

One honest limit on that inference: with a single seed per arm this shows the
*absence of a detectable* effect, not the absence of an effect.

## 5. N4 — counterfactual severity response

One fixed background, identical defect layout, severity the only variable
(`conf = 0.10`):

| severity | GT contrast | detections | mean conf |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.00 (clean) | 0.0 | **0** | — |
| 0.25 | 15.3 | 0 | — |
| 0.50 | 32.1 | 0 | — |
| 0.75 | 48.7 | 4 | 0.229 |
| 1.00 | 65.2 | 14 | 0.249 |

**Spearman ρ = 0.894** on detections, mean confidence, max confidence and
predicted area. **Zero false alarms on the clean canvas.** The detector responds
to defect *contrast*, not to layout or position.

Two honest qualifications: *p* = 0.10 is the **ceiling** at n = 5 rungs unless
ρ = 1.0 exactly (floor is 2/120 = 0.017), so the p-value is limited by rung
count. And there is a clear detection threshold at severity 0.5–0.75, matching
the measured T1 recall of ~0.04.

## 9. Robustness — where it breaks

Baseline mAP50 = 0.1773, mean conf = 0.1585 (val defect images).

**Gaussian noise is catastrophic and the model does not notice:**

| perturbation | mAP50 | Δ mAP | Δ conf |
|---|---|---|---|
| noise 0.02 | 0.0070 | **−96.1%** | only −13.5% |
| noise 0.05 | 0.0006 | **−99.7%** | only −14.4% |
| contrast −0.7 | 0.0017 | −99.0% | −54.0% ✓ |
| brightness −0.6 | 0.0294 | −83.4% | −37.4% ✓ |
| jpeg 0.85 | 0.1698 | −4.2% | +8.9% |

2% additive noise destroys the detector while it stays 86% as confident. For
FESEM this is an operational limit, not a curiosity — low-dwell-time
acquisition is noisy.

### How many conditions does the model fail to notice? 10 of 21

The flag in `eval/robustness.py` originally tested only the *direction* of the
confidence change, so any drop at all counted as "tracking". That passed
noise@0.02 — the worst failure in the sweep — as well-behaved, because
confidence did fall, by 13.5%, while mAP fell 96.1%.

The criterion now requires the confidence drop to reach **at least a third** of
the mAP drop, and separates the two distinct failure modes:

| mode | n | conditions |
|---|---|---|
| **confidence rose** while mAP fell | 4 | brightness_up@0.4, brightness_up@0.6, blur@0.2, downscale@0.5 |
| **silent failure** — mAP collapsed, confidence barely moved | 6 | contrast_down@−0.3, **noise@0.02**, **noise@0.05**, blur@0.4, blur@0.6, downscale@0.7 |
| well-behaved | 11 | — |

So **10 of 21 perturbations degrade the detector without it registering the
degradation**, not 4 as previously reported. For a triage tool whose value rests
on knowing when to defer to a human, this is the most operationally serious
finding in this document.

> The earlier "4/21 overconfident" figure was not wrong about those 4 — it was
> incomplete, because the test could not see a case where confidence moved the
> right way by a negligible amount. Recomputed from the stored deltas; no
> re-inference was needed.

## 10. Physics interpretation — indices, **not** measurements

Every quantity is image-derived. Renamed from the original code, which reported
them as physical facts.

| reported as | actually is |
|---|---|
| "Vertical Shunt Severity" | **Relative Depression Index (RDI)** — dimensionless; SfS min-max normalised to a *hard-coded* 500 nm assumption |
| "Fatal shunts: N" | *N regions flagged by RDI ≥ 0.75* — nothing electrical was measured |
| "GBDI = 0.92" | **Boundary Proximity Index** — mean Gaussian distance to Canny ridges, **not** a passivation fraction |
| "Voc loss = 4.75 mV" | **intensity-proxy ΔVoc index, arbitrary units** — no photoluminescence was ever measured |

Val, expert boxes: RDI n=198 mean 0.803 (**saturates** — median and p90 both
1.000, so as implemented it is not discriminative); BPI n=1 (only one val image
contains PbI₂); ΔVoc index mean 8.07 a.u.

> **No nm-per-pixel calibration exists for this corpus.** FESEM pixel-size
> headers were destroyed by JPEG re-encoding in all 440 source images and no TIF
> originals survive. Any depth in nm would be invented.

## 12. Limitations

- **Validation is thin:** 12 defect images, **1 PbI₂ image**. Seed spread on the
  baseline alone was 36% before replication.
- **PbI₂ is unmeasurable:** 5 train / 1 val image; AP = 0.0000 in every run and
  correctly flagged not-interpretable. No PbI₂ claim is made.
- **Open-set is not evaluable:** n_unknown = 1. AUROC 0.54 ≈ chance; AUPR 0.985
  is inflated by a 39:1 base rate. Needs annotation, not compute — the 65
  unlabelled PbI₂ images are the unlock.
- **Curve shape is single-seed** at 2%/10%/25%. Only the 5%-vs-baseline contrast
  is replicated.
- **The headline is step-confounded.** Equal epochs, unequal steps (8,300 vs
  2,000). Untested until §13 runs — this is the largest live threat to the
  central claim, not a footnote.
- **H2 is not supported at the detection level** (§3). Frequency realism
  improved 62% and detection did not move.
- **Explainability is not yet usable:** defect density ranges 0.3%–99.8% of frame
  across val images, so background-attribution ratio measures annotation density
  rather than model attention. n=3 cannot support a pointing-game estimate.
- **T1 remains unsolved:** best sub-stride recall is 0.044. This is the honest
  limit of a P3-stride detector on <8 px objects; the P2 ablation was never run.
- **Sizes are in pixels only** — see §10.

## 13. The step-matched control — resolved, and it overturned §1

Both original headline arms ran 100 epochs, but 660 vs 160 training images
means 8,300 vs 2,000 gradient steps, so the synthetic arm received 4.15× the
optimisation. This section existed to separate *synthetic data helped* from
*more steps helped*. It has now been run, and the answer is the second one.

**What was run.** The plan above matched *upward* — real-only at 8,300 steps,
415 epochs. That was replaced by matching *downward*: the synthetic arm at the
baseline's 2,000 steps, which is 24 epochs at 82 steps/epoch. Same question,
27 minutes instead of 2.2 hours, and no 415-epoch run on 160 images to
explain away as over-fitting.

```bash
for s in 1 2 42; do
  py -3.10 train_detector.py --regime scale_005_steps2000 --seed $s \
      --synth-pool refined --synth-ratio 0.05 --target-steps 2000
done
# each printed: step-budget: n_train=660 steps/epoch=82 -> epochs=24
# each experiments/scale_005_steps2000_yolo11s_seed<N>/run/results.csv: 24 rows
```

**Reading, fixed before the numbers existed** (the direction is reversed
from the earlier draft of this section; the thresholds are the same):

| `scale_005` at 2,000 steps, test mAP50 | conclusion |
|---|---|
| ≈ 0.13 | the 1.94× is **synthetic data** — headline stands |
| ≈ 0.09–0.11 | **part data, part compute** — report the matched ratio |
| ≈ 0.07 | the gain was **compute** — §1 rewritten |

**Result: 0.0662 ± 0.0186 against real-only 0.0688 ± 0.0105. Ratio 0.96×,
Welch p = 0.84.** Third row. At a matched step budget, adding 5% synthetic
data changes test mAP50 by nothing that three seeds can distinguish from zero.

**The caveat that travels with this.** 24 epochs is a short schedule for a
660-image set, so part of the null is under-training of the synthetic arm —
just as 415 epochs on 160 images would have partly measured over-fitting of
the baseline. Neither direction is a perfect control. But the burden was on
the synthetic arm to show a gain that survives *some* fair budget, and at the
one fair budget tested it shows none. §1 is rewritten accordingly.

## 14. Open-set — does a pinhole-only detector fire on PbI₂?

Three detectors trained on pinhole boxes only (`--known-classes 1`, seeds 1, 2,
42, 100 epochs); PbI₂ never seen in training and held out by the export. Scored
on the locked test split. `eval/open_set.py`, outputs in
`outputs/open_set_{val,test}_seed*.json`.

**The corpus cannot support the image-level design.** Test contains **3**
images whose only defects are PbI₂; val contains **1**. Image-level AUROC is
0.918 ± 0.037 on test with mean max-confidence 0.278 on pinhole images against
0.089 on PbI₂-only images — the direction is right and the number is
meaningless at n = 3. It is recorded, with the caveat string the script
writes, and no claim rests on it.

**Box level is what the corpus can support.** Every ground-truth box gets the
confidence of its best-overlapping detection (IoU ≥ 0.5), or 0 if none fires:
735 pinhole boxes as positives, 596 PbI₂ boxes as negatives.

| test, mean ± sd over 3 seeds | pinhole (known) | PbI₂ (unknown) |
|---|---|---|
| boxes | 735 | 596 |
| detected at conf ≥ 0.05 | **28.1% ± 3.8%** | **2.9% ± 1.2%** |
| detected at conf ≥ 0.10 | 18.3% ± 4.4% | 1.0% ± 0.8% |
| detected at conf ≥ 0.25 | 2.8% ± 2.0% | 0.0% |
| box-level AUROC | 0.576 ± 0.021 | |

Two things are true at once and must not be blurred together:

1. **The detector rejects the unknown class.** At the lowest usable threshold
   it fires on 3% of PbI₂ boxes against 28% of pinhole boxes — a 9:1 ratio —
   and on none at all by conf 0.25. A pinhole-only detector does not
   hallucinate pinholes on bright PbI₂ particles. That is the open-set answer,
   and it is a positive one.
2. **The detector has poor recall on its own class.** 72% of pinhole boxes get
   no detection even at conf 0.05. That is the closed-set recall problem from
   §1, not an open-set property.

The AUROC of 0.58 is the second fact contaminating the first: 72% of positives
and 97% of negatives tie at score 0, so the ranking statistic sits near chance
regardless of how well the detector separates the boxes it *does* fire on.
FPR@95TPR and FPR@50TPR are both 1.0 for the same reason — the threshold that
keeps 50% of pinholes is 0, which every box clears. The per-threshold rows are
the interpretable numbers; the AUROC is reported because it was pre-registered.

Closed-set pinhole mAP50 of these probes on test, pinhole-only vocabulary, 70
images: 0.1212 / 0.0757 / 0.0888 (mean 0.095 ± 0.023).

**A first draft of this section was wrong in a way worth recording.** It
reported a 0.0 false-alarm rate at conf 0.25 as perfect rejection. The known
recall at that threshold was 2.8%. A detector that fires on almost nothing has
a false-alarm rate of almost zero on everything; the number said nothing about
discrimination until it was placed beside the recall. The scoring was rewritten
to be threshold-free and to carry the recall next to every false-alarm rate.

## 15. Balanced synthetic at matched steps — slightly worse than no synthetic

The §13 control was run with PbI₂-only synthetic images (§0). This is the same
control with the sampling fixed: `scale_005_bal_steps2000`, 160 real + 500

| arm | synthetic mix | steps | mAP50 | mAP50-95 |

| real only @2,000 | 0.0096 | 0.0561 | 0.1084 | 0.1187 |
| PbI₂-only synth @2,000 | 0.0067 | 0.0589 | 0.1204 | 0.1000 |
| balanced synth @2,000 | 0.0079 | 0.0615 | **0.0948** | **0.0626** |

The loss sits in T3 and T4 — the larger defects — where the balanced arm is
12% and 47% below real-only. T1 and T2 are flat.

**Reading.** Adding PbI₂-only synthetic images did nothing (§13). Adding
synthetic *pinholes* as well makes pinhole detection worse, mainly on the
larger pinholes. That is negative transfer, and it points at one thing: the
rendered-and-refined pinhole does not look enough like a real one for the
detector to generalise from it, and the mismatch is largest for big pits,
where there is the most texture to get wrong. This is consistent with §5.3.5
of the proposal, where the morphology-level gap (L3) barely moved between
renderer output and refined output, and with the visible halo the refiner
leaves around large pinholes (Figure 8 there).

So for the question Phase 2 objective 5 asks — *does the synthetic data help?*
— the answer at a matched optimisation budget is **no, and the balanced
version is mildly harmful**, at n = 3 seeds and 24 epochs. The 2× in earlier
drafts was compute (§13); the ladder shape was class imbalance (§0).

**What this does not settle.** The same 24-epoch caveat as §13 applies. The
balanced arm has never been run at the 100-epoch / 8,300-step protocol, so the
2×2 (class mix × budget) has one empty cell; filling it costs 3 × 37 min and
would say whether balanced synthetic reaches the PbI₂-only arm's 0.1333 at the
same compute. And a synthetic-only balanced arm (§16, pending) will say whether
the pool carries *any* transferable signal on its own, which separates
"synthetic hurts when mixed in" from "synthetic is simply off-distribution".

## 16. Synthetic only — the pool carries no transferable signal

`synth_only_005_bal`: 500 synthetic images (12,960 PbI₂ + 13,000 pinhole
boxes), **no real training images**, real val for checkpoint selection. Seeds
1, 2, 42. Scored on the locked test split.

| arm | train images | test mAP50 | mAP50-95 | recall | precision |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| real only | 160 real | 0.0688 ± 0.0105 | 0.0261 | 0.282 | 0.032 |
| balanced mixed @2,000 (§15) | 160 real + 500 synth | 0.0533 ± 0.0033 | 0.0182 | 0.258 | 0.022 |
| **balanced synthetic only** | **500 synth** | **0.0029 ± 0.0023** | **0.0009** | **0.054** | **0.004** |

Per seed: 0.0010 / 0.0023 / 0.0055. Per-bin AP50 is ≤ 0.009 in every bin.

**The training curves say why.** Train box loss fell normally (1.70 → 1.05):
the detector learned the synthetic pool. Val mAP50 on *real* images peaked at
**epoch 1–4** (0.010 – 0.016) and decayed toward zero from there; all three
runs early-stopped at 31 – 43 epochs on patience 30. The selected `best.pt`
is therefore the COCO-pretrained initialisation with a few epochs of drift.
Fitting the synthetic data was monotonically harmful to real performance.

**Reading, against §15.** §15 asked whether mixing synthetic in hurts, or
whether the synthetic is simply off-distribution. This answers it: the pool
on its own transfers **nothing**, so the mild loss in §15 is 500
off-distribution images diluting 160 real ones, not an interaction effect. The
generator produces images a detector can fit and cannot use.

That is the end of the synthetic-data line as it stands. Across §13, §15, §16:

| question | answer, locked test, n = 3 |
|---|---|
| does 5% synthetic help at matched compute? | no — 0.96× (PbI₂-only), 0.78× (balanced) |
| does synthetic help on its own? | no — 0.04× |
| does *real-appearance* copy-paste help? (§17) | no — 0.76×, same as rendered |
| was the earlier 2× real? | it was 4.15× the gradient steps |

What remains true and useful: the labels are exact, the geometry matches, the
severity control works, the leakage-safe protocol stands, and the H2 result —
that the FFT branch closes the *frequency* gap while changing nothing
downstream — is now one instance of a broader finding: **none of the three
measured gap levels predicted this**. L1 pixel and L2 frequency both improved
under refinement; the detector did not care. The plan's §8.2 pre-registered
"no gap level predicts utility" as a reportable outcome. This is it.

**Next, if the synthetic line continues.** The failure is on the *pinhole*
appearance specifically (§15 per-bin: the loss is in T3/T4 pinholes). The
morphology gap L3 never moved under refinement (§5.3.5 of the proposal). The
refiner was trained on 192-px patches with a mask-area-normalised L1 against
real defect crops; the halo it leaves on large pits (proposal Figure 8) is the
visible symptom. A refiner change is the lever, not more data or more seeds.

> **Superseded by §17.** The paragraph above was written before the copy-paste
> control ran. That control uses *real* defect appearance and lands exactly
> where the rendered pool did, so appearance was not the lever. Kept in place
> so the reasoning chain stays auditable.

## 17. Copy-paste control — real appearance, same result

§15–16 blamed the rendered pinhole's appearance. The decisive test is a pool
with **no rendering at all**: real annotated defect crops from the train split,
Poisson-blended onto real defect-free train backgrounds, with the box copied
verbatim (`synth/copypaste.py`, Ghiasi et al. 2021). Labels are exact, the
appearance is real by construction, and the two composition faults of the
rendered pool are fixed as well: per-image defect count is *sampled* from the
real prior instead of pinned to its mean, and 40% of images are backgrounds.
Pool: 500 images (220 backgrounds), 3,845 PbI₂ + 3,903 pinhole boxes cut from
2,607 real train boxes.

Pre-registered before the run: **≥ 0.08 → appearance was the lever; ≈ 0.07 →
a task/evaluation floor; below real-only → the loss is not appearance.**

Same protocol as §15: 160 real + 500 copy-paste, 2,000 steps = 24 epochs,
seeds 1, 2, 42, YOLO11s, locked test split.

| arm | synthetic appearance | steps | mAP50 | mAP50-95 | recall | precision |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| real only | — | 2,000 | 0.0688 ± 0.0105 | 0.0261 ± 0.0053 | 0.282 | 0.032 |
| + 5% rendered + refined (§15) | GAN | 2,000 | 0.0533 ± 0.0033 | 0.0182 ± 0.0016 | 0.258 | 0.022 |
| **+ 500 copy-paste** | **real crops** | **2,000** | **0.0525 ± 0.0052** | **0.0169 ± 0.0024** | **0.254** | **0.029** |

Per seed, copy-paste = [0.0468, 0.0571, 0.0536]. Against real-only: **0.76×,
Welch p = 0.097**. Against the rendered pool: **0.98×, p = 0.83** — the two
synthetic arms are indistinguishable.

| per-bin AP50, test | T1 <8 px | T2 8–16 | T3 16–32 | T4 ≥32 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| real only @2,000 | 0.0096 | 0.0561 | 0.1084 | 0.1187 |
| rendered @2,000 (§15) | 0.0079 | 0.0615 | 0.0948 | 0.0626 |
| copy-paste @2,000 | 0.0091 | 0.0630 | **0.0605** | 0.0977 |

Same shape as §15: T1/T2 flat, the loss in T3/T4. Which bin takes the larger
hit differs between the two pools, and with n = 3 that difference is inside
seed spread.

**Reading: the third pre-registered outcome.** A pool made of real defects on
real backgrounds hurts by the same amount as a pool made of rendered ones. So
the §15–16 diagnosis — that the *rendered pinhole does not look real enough* —
is not what the numbers were measuring. Whatever these 500 images cost the
detector, they cost it whether the pixels were synthesised or cut from
annotated real images. The remaining explanations are the ones shared by both
pools:

1. **Budget dilution.** At a fixed 2,000 steps, 660 training images means each
   real image is visited 24 times instead of 100. The synthetic images do not
   just add signal; they displace passes over the 160 real ones. Under this
   reading no 500-image pool of *any* quality can win at matched steps, and
   the fair comparison is the empty cell of the 2×2 — balanced or copy-paste
   at 8,300 steps against real-only at 8,300 steps (§15, "what this does not
   settle"). That cell is still empty.
2. **Composition is still off.** Copy-paste fixed the constant-count and
   no-background faults, so those two are not the cause. What both pools
   still share is *placement*: defects scattered by the grain-boundary prior
   over backgrounds that a human labelled as defect-free, at counts sampled
   from images that were chosen for having defects. A background image with
   26 pasted pits is not a real defective image; it is a clean film with
   holes in it.
3. **A task floor.** Real-only mAP50 is 0.069 ± 0.010 on 22 defect-bearing
   test images with a 3-class-merged label set where 34% of boxes are under
   8 px. The seed spread alone is 15% of the mean; a 24% effect is one and a
   half seed-spreads.

### Val and test disagree on every synthetic seed

The checkpoint of every run is selected on the real val split. On val, both
synthetic arms **beat** real-only on every seed; on test they lose on every
seed:

| run | val mAP50 (selection) | test mAP50 (locked) |
|---|---|---|
| real only s1 / s2 / s42 | 0.072 / 0.065 / 0.051 | 0.079 / 0.069 / 0.058 |
| rendered @2,000 s1 / s2 / s42 | 0.087 / 0.073 / **0.093** | 0.057 / 0.051 / 0.051 |
| copy-paste s1 / s2 / s42 | 0.077 / 0.082 / 0.066 | 0.047 / 0.057 / 0.054 |

Real-only: val mean 0.063, test 0.069. Synthetic arms: val mean 0.080, test
0.053. Val has 12 defect-bearing images (640 boxes, one of them carrying all
108 PbI₂ boxes); test has 22 (1,331 boxes). Two things follow. First, **any
claim in this document that rests on a val number is unreliable for the
synthetic arms** — the val gain is real and does not transfer. Second, the
best-epoch selection is itself tuned toward the val images, and with 12 of
them the synthetic arms may be selecting a checkpoint that happens to fit val.
This is the argument for grouped cross-validation over the 84 defect groups
that the plan's §20 listed as a fallback; at n = 3 seeds and 12 val images it
is no longer optional if the synthetic line is pursued further.

**Where this leaves objective 5.** Three synthetic pools (PbI₂-only rendered,
balanced rendered, copy-paste), one protocol, nine seeds: none improves
detection on the locked test split at matched compute, and the two that carry
pinholes hurt by about a quarter. The failure is not the generator's
appearance. It is either the budget split, the composition, or the floor of
a 160-image task — and the experiment that separates those is the
8,300-step 2×2, not another generator.

### Pre-registered: the 8,300-step cell (written before the runs)

Six runs, seeds 1, 2, 42, YOLO11s, locked test split: `real_only_steps8300`
(160 real, 415 epochs, patience 500) and `scale_005_bal_steps8300` (160 real +
500 balanced rendered, 101 epochs, patience 500). Both arms take 8,300
gradient steps, so the synthetic images no longer displace passes over the
real ones. The rendered pool is used rather than copy-paste because objective
5 is about this project's generator, and at 2,000 steps the two pools were
indistinguishable (0.98×, above).

| if, at 8,300 steps | then §17 ends |
|---|---|
| synthetic ≥ real-only by more than seed spread (ratio ≥ ~1.15, Welch p < 0.05) | *synthetic augmentation failed at 2,000 steps mainly because matched-step training diluted exposure to the real images; given the budget to fit both, it helps* — and the 0.1333 in §1 was partly transfer after all |
| synthetic ≈ real-only (ratio within 0.85–1.15 or p ≥ 0.05) | *synthetic augmentation remained ineffective after removing the training-budget confound* — the pool is neutral at any budget; the 2× in §1 was compute, full stop |
| synthetic < real-only by more than seed spread | *ineffective and harmful at any budget* — composition or off-distribution, and the §16 synthetic-only 0.04× is the same effect undiluted |

Also read: whether real-only at 8,300 steps rises above its 2,000-step 0.0688.
If it does, 100 epochs was under-trained and every real-only number in this
document is a floor, not a ceiling.

Whichever row lands, **Phase 2 is frozen at these six runs.** No further arms.

|---|---|---|---|---|

**YOLOv8s vs YOLO11s.** 0.083 against a YOLO11s seed range of 0.058 – 0.079:
at the top of the range, one seed, not a finding. Both are the same family at
the same budget; the honest statement is that the two are interchangeable here.

**P2 head — ablation A4, confounded, reported anyway.** The plan predicted the
stride-4 level would help T1/T2. It did not: T1 is unchanged at 0.008 and every
other bin is a third of the plain model. But the comparison is not clean:

- Ultralytics 8.4.127 ships no `yolo11-p2` topology, so the pair is YOLOv8s,
  not YOLO11s. (`train_detector.py` now fails fast on this instead of after
  wiping the experiment directory, which is how the first attempt found out.)
- The P2 model is built from YAML; the COCO checkpoint transfers into
  **219 of 437** tensors — the backbone — and the P2 neck and all four
  detection heads start from random. The plain model starts fully pretrained.
- It early-stopped at epoch 71 with train cls-loss still at 2.5 against ~1.0
  for the plain model, and val mAP50 still rising. It is under-trained.

So this row says *a P2 head trained from a partially pretrained start, for 71
epochs, on 160 images, is worse*. It does not say the stride-4 level cannot
help. A fair A4 needs either a P2-pretrained checkpoint (none published) or a
longer schedule with the neck warmed up first; it is the one ablation in the
plan that this environment cannot run cleanly, and that goes in Limitations.

**RT-DETR-L.** Val peaked at epoch ~37 and decayed; test precision 0.017 at
recall 0.28 means it fires almost everywhere. 32 M parameters, 7× the
training time, 160 images: the expected outcome for a transformer detector at
this data scale, and the reason the plan's §7.4 hedged on RF-DETR/D-FINE. The
1.6× T2 AP over YOLO11s s42 sits inside the YOLO11s seed range.

**MicroDefectCV** (§6) is two orders of magnitude below every trained model on
mAP50 at 22,922 predictions over 22 images; its recall at any usable precision
is nil. R1's question — how far does zero-training morphology get — is
answered: not far, on this corpus.
